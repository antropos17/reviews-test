Transparency rules

These rules explain the service's current behavior. This badge is not a certification of software quality or security.

Ratings and reviews

Each eligible GitHub user may maintain one rating and one independent review per public repository. Ratings from accounts younger than 30 days are held until eligible. The average is the arithmetic mean of eligible ratings. Repository owners cannot rate or review their own repositories; their earlier ratings do not count. Organization ratings require an installed GitHub App with read-only organization members permission. Current organization owners are excluded. If GitHub cannot verify ownership or access is revoked, ratings and new feedback are unavailable until verification succeeds. Existing reviews remain visible and all stored feedback is retained. A person's other accounts cannot be reliably identified.

Directory and ranking

Directory scores use the same eligible rating rules as project pages. Top rated requires at least five eligible ratings and excludes service test repositories. Projects are ordered by average, then rating count, then repository name. This is an arithmetic ranking, not a confidence guarantee. Reviews and ratings are independent: a project may have reviews without ratings. Hidden review revisions and their associated ratings are excluded. Unknown or unavailable ratings never become a zero score.

First-hand authorship

New reviews and edits require a declaration that the author used the project and wrote the review without AI-generated text or an automated agent. This declaration is recorded with the signed review event. It is a statement by the author, not proof that a person wrote the text. Existing reviews are preserved. No AI detector is used to claim certainty about authorship. Reports of manipulation are reviewed under the moderation rules below.

Security evidence

Community Feedback does not currently scan source code, dependencies or release artifacts. Security is shown as not assessed. A linked security concern is an unverified claim, not a confirmed vulnerability. OpenSSF Scorecard and repository advisories open external evidence; their coverage, date and exact revision must be checked there. Absence of advisories or a high community score never means malware-free software. A future security assessment must identify the commit, scanner version, scope, time and evidence separately from the community rating.

Reports and moderation

Signed-in accounts at least 30 days old may report a specific version of a review for spam, abuse, personal information, or another concern. Reports do not automatically hide reviews or change ratings. Reports are not public; designated service moderators and administrators can access them, including reporter identities. Repository owners cannot moderate their own repositories, and moderators cannot decide their own reports or reviews.

Only a designated service moderator may decide a report. Hiding requires a reason: spam, personal data, harassment, or a security claim without evidence. Decisions are recorded in the public moderation log without the hidden review text. When no independent moderator is designated, reports remain pending. A review edited after a report becomes a new version.

A rating associated with a hidden review is excluded while that review is hidden. An individual repository owner can post one public reply per review. A security concern must include a link to a file, line, issue or commit in the same repository; the service checks the link format, not the truth of the claim.

Seal and journal

A GitHub administrator can activate the seal. A scheduled check records the first observed removal and the rating at that time; failed GitHub checks are not proof of removal. Feedback remains public. New ratings and their GitHub account IDs are public in the signed journal. Review events publish a hash and its salt for verification: predictable hidden text could be guessed. Service-signed receipts can be checked against a separately retained copy. Older feedback does not have retroactive signed receipts.

Limits and accountability

GitHub sign-in verifies an account, not a unique person. The service cannot prevent all coordinated manipulation. The service operator controls its hosting, code, database, signing key and moderator configuration; privileged administrators can alter data or logs. A local mirror is not an independent witness and does not automatically check the displayed score or reviews. The public log currently shows the latest 100 decisions. No project may buy a higher score or pay to remove critical feedback under these rules.

Contact and appeal arrangements are not yet in place. Do not include private information in a report or review.
